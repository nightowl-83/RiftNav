## 2. The brief, as it evolved

**Original ask.** Mike had an 8-second looping video background made with Higgsfield + Midjourney and
wanted it to respond to user actions: load in → idle waiting loop → pan/rotate on selection →
return on exit. His instinct was to generate one long video containing every state and seek to
timestamps.

**Second version.** Particles morph into a form resembling the selected item type (rift = tear in
space), with scan lines and readout text emanating from it, then an ambient loop on that form.

**Final version (the one that stands).** Full concept, in Mike's words:

> The initial state is a galaxy. The idle state is a very slow, ambient rotation that loops
> seamlessly and smooth. Particles should be as small as possible.
>
> Two triggers: (1) user selects a browse option — "browse rifts", "browse dig sites";
> (2) user searches for a specific event, rift, or dig site by name.
>
> All "Astral Rifts" transition into the Rift background. All "Archaeology" events transition to
> the "Dig site" background. Once transitioned, subtle slow ambient motion, settling, seamless loop.
>
> Consider options where it isn't only reordering the points, but also **zooming into an area** as
> the points are reordering.
>
> The final goal should feel as though the user is interacting with a highly advanced mapping system
> of the galaxy, and searching for an item. Once located, the background acts as though it is
> providing holographic imagery of the item found.
>
> All interactions that change the background should have a "reverse" animation that returns the
> user to the top-level galaxy starting point.

"Events" are explicitly **deferred** — focus on rifts and dig sites only.

## 4. The architecture decision that still holds

**Do not build this as generated video. Build it as a canvas/WebGL particle system.**

The original plan was a state graph of short generated clips with pixel-matched junction frames.
That spec is written and still exists as a fallback (`Holo Deck Motion Spec — v1.3.html`), but the
canvas route wins decisively:

- **Click latency disappears.** With video, a transition can only start at a loop boundary — up to
  3 seconds of the user clicking and nothing happening. This was the single worst problem in the
  video design. A particle system morphs on the click frame.
- **"Seamless loop" becomes free.** There is no loop to close; it is a continuous simulation.
- **Arbitrary transitions cost nothing.** Rift → dig direct, per-entity variation, zoom — all free.
  Video needed an n² scope guard.
- **~15 KB of JS vs ~20 MB of video**, and responsive rather than a fixed 16:9 crop.
- No generation credits, no iteration lottery, no drift.

What video would still win: volumetric light, depth of field, film-like texture. Weakened by the
fact that `videoDim` throws most of it away.

**Also decided: scan lines and readout text go in the DOM, never baked into the imagery.**
AI video renders text as flickering garbage glyphs; the real data already exists in the JSON; and
baking data into pixels puts it out of reach of screen readers. The particle layer carries light;
HTML/SVG draws leader lines and labels. This also means a "retarget" (switching between two rifts)
is a pure overlay change with no background transition at all.

## 5. Prototype state

`Holo/prototype-v4-galaxy.html` — also published at
`https://claude.ai/code/artifact/f152b4a7-53ee-4535-a733-fedefc541978`

Canvas 2D, sprite + fillRect rendering, ~9000 particles default (14000 ceiling). Implements:

- **Galaxy form** — 4-arm log spiral, core bulge, thin disc, sparse halo. Colour by radius:
  white core → amber bar → blue arms. Differential rotation (`ω ∝ 1/r`) so arms shear.
- **Rift form** — sphere with longitude quantised into 72 meridians (produces the vertical
  scanlines of R7), limb brightening for the hot rim, a few latitude rings.
- **Dig form** — ground quantised into 64 rows on a noise function, 24 cone markers, low oblique camera.
- **Dive** — every catalogue item has a stable galaxy coordinate derived by hashing its name.
  Camera flies to that coordinate; the object assembles *there*. Return reverses it.
- **Both triggers** — browse buttons and a search field.
- **Trails** via canvas persistence (low-alpha fill instead of clear) rather than clearing each frame.
- Live art-direction controls: trails, flow, density, videoDim, holoScan, scan overlay, fps.

Earlier prototypes in this session, in order: column of particles → column with size/colour
variation and hairlines → particle sphere with trails → this galaxy version.

## 6. Techniques worth keeping regardless of direction

These were learned the hard way and apply to any particle implementation:

- **Trails via persistence.** Do not `clearRect`. Paint `rgba(bg, 0.05–0.3)` over the frame instead.
  Every particle smears into a fading ribbon. This is what makes R1/R2/R5 look expensive, and it is
  nearly free.
- **Power-law sizes.** `size ∝ r^3` — most particles sub-pixel, a few large. A field of
  identically-sized dots always reads as low-resolution no matter how many there are.
- **Render small particles as crisp `fillRect`, not scaled sprites.** A soft radial gradient scaled
  to 1px is mush. Sprites only for the large minority, where they carry the bloom.
- **Group draws by colour** so `fillStyle` is set 4–6 times per frame, not thousands.
- **Limb brightening** — brighten particles where a shell turns away from the viewer. This is what
  makes a cloud of dots read as a *sphere*.
- **Project the flow field onto the tangent plane** (subtract the component along the surface normal)
  so trails hug the surface and curl instead of flying outward.
- **Let a small fraction escape** (~5–16%) with a weak spring — the wisps and tendrils around the
  outside are a large part of why references feel alive.
- **Quantise a coordinate to make scanlines.** Longitude → vertical meridians; z → terrain rows.
  This is how R7 and R8 get their texture.
- **Differential rotation** for anything disc-shaped. Uniform rotation reads as a spinning graphic.

## 7. What went wrong — read this first

Mike's verdict on the galaxy prototype: **"Not really what I am looking for."** No specifics were
given before he switched models, so the following is inference, not fact. Treat it as hypotheses to
test, not conclusions.

Candidate problems, most likely first:

1. **The galaxy probably does not look like R6.** R6 is a photographic Hubble-style galaxy — dense
   luminous gas, dust lanes, thousands of resolved stars, an intensely blown-out core. 9000 discrete
   canvas points cannot reach that. It likely reads as a thin spiral of dots, not a galaxy. This is
   the most probable failure and it is a *fidelity* gap, not a design one.
   → Likely fixes: a genuinely photographic core glow (layered radial gradients, not particles);
   far more particles via WebGL; additive nebula sprites for the arms; or compositing a real galaxy
   image underneath the particle layer.
2. **The three states may be too visually unrelated.** Galaxy (warm, photographic) → teal wireframe
   sphere → teal terrain. They may not feel like one system.
3. **Canvas 2D may simply be the wrong tool** for this fidelity target. WebGL with additive blending,
   real bloom post-processing and 100k+ points is the honest next step if the look matters more than
   the build cost. This was flagged as an option twice and never taken.
4. **The whole canvas premise may be wrong** and Mike may want the generated-video richness after
   all. The video spec is intact if so.

**Process note for the next session:** a lot of effort went into architecture, specs and prompt
packs before there was anything to look at. The fastest useful loop here was *build it and show him*.
Do that earlier. Also — Claude could not view Higgsfield's generated images at all (see §8), which
made the video route effectively unreviewable and is part of why canvas was favoured.
